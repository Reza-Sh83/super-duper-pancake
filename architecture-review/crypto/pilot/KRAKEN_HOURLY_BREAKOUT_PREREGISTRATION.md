# Preregistration for one hourly Kraken Spot BTC/USD breakout experiment

Owner is Architect. Question: on a fixed forward historical window, how does a delayed, long-only 24-hour breakout with a 12-hour exit compare with cash and buy-and-hold after fees? The signal is fixed as a simple range rule with no fitted parameters. This is a paper bar proxy. The existing weekly study keeps its own frozen schedule. Shared calendar dates can produce dependent outcomes across the studies.

## Signal and modeled fills

At open of hour t, the most recent eligible bar started at t−2h and closed at t−1h. If flat and eligible to enter, buy when that close is **strictly above** the highest high of the preceding 24 bars, t−26h through t−3h inclusive. If long, exit when that close is **strictly below** the lowest low of the preceding 12 bars, t−14h through t−3h inclusive. Equality leaves the position unchanged. A risk exit takes precedence and no exit and re-entry occur at the same open. The signal bar is excluded from both reference ranges. Ignore the fill bar's eventual high, low and close until the appropriate clock event. The one-hour buffer avoids assigning a fill to the signal-close boundary.

Simulate a full fill at that hour's open. Base uses 0.8% taker fee each side, zero added slippage. Stress uses 1% each side and 10 bp adverse slippage each side. Buy price is open × (1 + slippage), sell price open × (1 − slippage), with fees on executed notional. Include no extra spread beyond the stated assumptions. Use Decimal precision 34, fractional BTC, no borrowing, no short and no top-up. Each breakout entry spends at most 2.5% of pretrade marked NAV, including the fee, and must satisfy the 10% post-trade exposure cap. In detail, with pretrade NAV N, executed per-BTC cash cost a and net liquidation mark m, choose quantity min(cash/a, 0.025N/a, 0.10N/[a+0.10(a−m)]) for a flat entry. Exposure uses gross open reference price for passive-cap detection and gross cost including fee as the conservative entry test. If pre-action gross position is above 10% of NAV at a later open, sell the excess to that cap using the open liquidation mark. P&L and the hold comparisons use the same mark convention.

## Risk and controls

Every arm starts with USD 10,000. For the strategy, sample net-liquidation NAV before the hourly open action, after the action and at hourly close. Close precedes next-open action at a shared timestamp. Mark as cash plus BTC times reference price times (1 − slippage) × (1 − fee). Sample 1% loss from the current day's opening pretrade NAV and 4% drawdown from running sampled peak, both inclusive. Latch either permanently in this experiment. Cancel increases immediately and liquidate at the next eligible open, or at the current open if the opening mark triggered the latch. Compute days strictly by UTC. Start the sampled high-water mark at USD 10,000. Keep terminal holdings marked with liquidation reserve and no forced terminal fill.

Under each cost model report four arms on matched bars: breakout, cash, 2.5%-of-initial-cash buy-and-hold including entry fee, and conventional fully invested buy-and-hold including entry fee. The hold controls buy once at the first eligible modeled open and hold to the final mark. They do not apply strategy stops or cap trims. They are passive benchmarks rather than candidates for pilot operation. Report exposures so different risk budgets are visible. No extra tuning, alternative windows or model selection after observing results.

## Verification and reporting

Before evaluation test strict range boundaries, a known breakout and exit, and invariance to changes in signal-bar high/low for its own reference range. Mutate t−1 and t candles and verify no effect on the t decision or t fill except its open price for fill and sizing. Reject missing warm-up and window bars. Check hand-calculated fees and terminal liquidation reserve under both costs, UTC midnight accounting without overlap, inclusive loss/drawdown latch precedence, zero cash-control turnover and exact endpoint exclusion. Freeze code, tests and source expectations in an exact Git commit before opening evaluation rows. Preserve all outcome rows and stop evidence privately.

Publish all arms including losses. Report net USD and percent, actual modeled fees, turnover, fills, liquidation reserve, sampled drawdown, exposure, locks and coverage. Primary descriptive comparison is stress net return versus the 2.5% hold, with full hold, cash and base results shown alongside. Drawdown and risk are observed only at the declared samples. OHLC lacks actual bid/ask, depth, IOC outcomes, receive-time availability and intrahour stop execution. For each complete UTC day use start and end marks at fixed boundaries, with the closing mark before the following open action. Net day return is the change in net NAV divided by opening NAV, inclusive of modeled charges. Report the distribution, best and worst days and the count at or above **50% net**. Even a modeled threshold crossing would be exploratory evidence and cannot trigger a pilot alert or count as validated execution. No partial-day claims.
